DESIGN PROJECT · 2026 FSAE · AERODYNAMICS LEAD

From wing concept to vehicle package.

The aero package was developed for low-speed competition, balancing cornering downforce with drag, packaging, and fabrication constraints. Work moved from wing airfoil and angle-of-attack trade-offs through front and rear wing layouts, body panels, and full-vehicle CAD.

Eppler 423 · multi-element wingsThree-element rear wing~10° working angle310 N · Cd 0.59 at 80 kph · simulated
What I worked throughCompared low-speed lift and drag trends, with an 8°–12° rear-wing angle range and about 10° as the working point before drag rose sharply. The upper two elements were designed to adjust for event conditions. The final team report records a full-package SolidWorks Flow result; it is model output, not measured car performance.

Wing balance

The final report identifies Eppler 423 for the multi-element front and rear wings. Rear-wing polar review compared 8°–12°; about 10° was the working point, with the upper elements adjustable for setup.

Packaging and fabrication

The rear-wing support evolved from side-plate mounts to a centered mount. The front wing shifted from a spoon concept to straighter multi-element sections. Body panels used foam or 3D-printed cores with planned carbon-fiber skins; side pods and skirts also directed airflow toward cooling.

Simulation result

The 2026 final report records 310 N downforce and a 0.59 drag coefficient at 80 kph from SolidWorks Flow. The aero report describes its flow visualization as preliminary and inconclusive, so this remains a reported simulation result, not track-validated performance.

737 winglet performance study

Compared cruise fuel-burn data for a 737-800 without winglets and a 737 MAX 8 with split-scimitar winglets using summary plots, a binary-predictor regression, and residual diagnostics. The report describes an approximately 330 kg/h group difference, but engine and airframe changes are confounding, so it does not isolate winglet-only savings.
